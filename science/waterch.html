2. The temperature graph

Open full page

Key ideas

  • The graph is flat while the state is changing: at 0 °C (melting or freezing) and at 100 °C (boiling).
  • During melting and boiling, water is still gaining heat, but its temperature does not change until the change of state is complete.
  • Boiling makes bubbles all through the water. Steam is an invisible gas; the white “cloud” you see is tiny droplets formed when steam condenses.

Try it

  • Heat the ice and watch the graph. Stop heating, then put it in the freezer. Point to where the line is flat and say what is happening.
Question: Ice is being heated. Its temperature stays at 0 °C for 5 minutes. Is it gaining heat? Explain.

Yes. It is gaining heat to melt. The temperature stays at 0 °C until all the ice has melted.

3. What makes water evaporate faster?

Faster evaporation when there is

  • Higher temperature
  • Larger exposed surface area
  • Wind (moving air)
  • Lower humidity (drier air)

Try it

  • Pick each factor and wait 2 hours twice. Only one thing is different between A and B each time. This is a fair test.
Question: Why do wet clothes dry faster on a sunny, windy day?

The higher temperature and the wind both increase the rate of evaporation, so the water in the clothes evaporates faster.

The natural water cycle

4. The water cycle

Open full page
Sun heats water→Evaporation→Condensation (clouds)→Precipitation (rain)→Collection (rivers, sea)

Key ideas

  • The water cycle is driven by the sun, which provides heat.
  • Water vapour rises, touches cooler air, loses heat and condenses into tiny droplets that form clouds.
  • The droplets grow heavy and fall as precipitation (rain or snow).
  • Importance: a continuous supply of fresh water for living things.

Try it

  • Watch a full cycle. Then switch the sun off: no new water evaporates, and the cycle stops.

Exam skills

6. Practice questions

Practice 1: Water droplets form on the outside of a glass of iced water. Explain why.

Water vapour in the surrounding air touches the cooler surface of the glass, loses heat and condenses into water droplets.

Practice 2: Give two differences between boiling and evaporation.

Boiling happens only at 100 °C, but evaporation happens at any temperature. Boiling takes place throughout the liquid, but evaporation takes place only at the surface. (Also: boiling is fast, evaporation is slow.)

Practice 3: A puddle dries up faster on a sunny day than on a cloudy day. Why?

On a sunny day, the temperature is higher, so the water gains heat faster and evaporates faster.

Practice 4: Explain how clouds form.

Water evaporates and the water vapour rises. Higher up, it touches cooler air, loses heat and condenses into tiny water droplets. Many droplets together form a cloud.

Practice 5: Why is the water cycle important to living things?

It ensures a continuous supply of fresh water for living things.
