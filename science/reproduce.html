Pollination · fertilisation · fruits and seeds

1. Inside a flower

Open full page
PartMale / femaleFunction
AntherMaleProduces and holds pollen grains (male reproductive cells).
FilamentMaleHolds up the anther so wind or insects can reach the pollen.
StigmaFemaleSticky surface that receives pollen grains.
StyleFemaleTube connecting the stigma to the ovary.
OvaryFemaleContains the ovules (female reproductive cells).

The process

  • Pollination: transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma. Agents: wind, insects, birds.
  • Fertilisation: the male reproductive cell fuses with the female reproductive cell in the ovule (a pollen tube grows down the style to reach it).
  • After fertilisation: ovary → fruit; ovule → seed. The petals, stamens, stigma and style wither.

Try it

  • Pick each part to hear its job. Then pollinate (by an insect or by the wind), fertilise, and wait two weeks. Count the ovules and then the seeds.
Question: Pollination has happened, but no fruit forms. Why?

Fertilisation did not take place, so the ovary did not develop into a fruit. Pollination alone is not enough.

Seed dispersal

2. Spreading the seeds

Open full page

Why disperse?

To prevent competition for light, water, space and mineral salts with the parent plant and with each other.

MethodFeatures of the fruit or seedExamples
WindLight; wing-like parts or fine hairsAngsana, dandelion, shorea
WaterFibrous husk traps air so it floats; waterproof skinCoconut, pong pong (sea mango)
AnimalsJuicy, brightly coloured (eaten); or hooks that stick to furRambutan, chilli, love grass
Explosive actionDry pods split open suddenly and fling the seedsBalsam, rubber seed

Try it

  • Try “no dispersal” first and let the seedlings grow: they are crowded and weak. Then try each way and compare how many seedlings have space.
Germination

3. What seeds need to germinate

Open full page
WOW: Water, Oxygen, Warmth. Light is NOT needed for germination! (A seedling needs light later, once its leaves start making food.)

Try it

  • Wait one day at a time. Lift the boxes to look at D (fridge) and E (dark). Which set-ups germinated? What was missing in each of the others?
Question: Why was the water in set-up C boiled and then covered with oil?

Boiling removes the dissolved air (oxygen) from the water, and the layer of oil stops air from dissolving back in. This makes sure the seeds have no oxygen.

Question: The seedlings in E are tall, thin and yellow. Did they germinate?

Yes. They germinated without light, which shows light is not needed for germination. They are yellow because they need light to make chlorophyll and food as they grow.

Non-flowering plants

4. Ferns and mosses: spores

Open full page

Key ideas

  • Non-flowering plants such as ferns and mosses do not produce flowers, fruits or seeds.
  • They reproduce by spores, made in spore bags (sporangia) on the underside of the leaves.
  • Spores are tiny and light and are dispersed by the wind. They grow in damp, shady places.
  • Fungi (such as mushrooms and bread mould) also reproduce by spores, but fungi are not plants.

Exam skills

6. Practice questions

Practice 1: What is the difference between pollination and fertilisation?

Pollination is the transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma. Fertilisation is the fusion of the male reproductive cell with the female reproductive cell in the ovule. Pollination happens first.

Practice 2: A fruit has 12 seeds. How many ovules were fertilised? Explain.

At least 12. Each seed develops from one fertilised ovule.

Practice 3: Why is it important for seeds to be dispersed away from the parent plant?

So that the young plants do not compete with the parent plant and with one another for light, water, space and mineral salts, and so the plant can grow in new places.

Practice 4: A flower has dull petals, no scent, and anthers that hang outside the flower. How is it likely pollinated? Explain.

By wind. It does not need bright petals or scent to attract insects, and the hanging anthers let the wind blow the pollen away.

Practice 5: Ali puts bean seeds on damp cotton wool in a dark cupboard. They germinate. What does this show?

Light is not needed for germination. The seeds had water, oxygen (air) and warmth.
