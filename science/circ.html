Respiratory system

1. Breathing

Open full page

Key ideas

  • Main organs: nose → windpipe (trachea) → lungs (full of tiny air sacs).
  • Breathing in (inhalation): air rich in oxygen is taken into the lungs. Oxygen passes through the air sac walls into the blood.
  • Breathing out (exhalation): blood carries carbon dioxide back to the lungs. Carbon dioxide passes into the air sacs and is breathed out.

Try it

  • Breathe in and out and watch the lungs and diaphragm. Then look at an air sac to see oxygen going into the blood and carbon dioxide coming out.
Question: Why do the lungs have millions of tiny air sacs instead of one big bag?

Many tiny air sacs give a very large surface area, so more oxygen can pass into the blood at the same time.

Question: Which gas is there more of in the air we breathe out than in the air we breathe in?

Carbon dioxide (and water vapour). There is less oxygen in the air we breathe out.

Circulatory system

2. Heart and blood

Open full page
PartWhat it does
HeartA muscular organ that acts as a pump, pushing blood continuously to all parts of the body.
BloodCarries oxygen, digested food, water and carbon dioxide (and other wastes) around the body.
Blood vesselsTubes that carry blood: arteries (away from the heart), veins (back to the heart), capillaries (tiny vessels inside organs).

Blood flow pathways

Rich in oxygen:Lungs→Heart→Rest of body
Rich in carbon dioxide:Rest of body→Heart→Lungs

Try it

  • Pick “oxygen-rich blood”, then “carbon dioxide-rich blood” and trace each path. Name the blood vessels. Notice the yellow specks: digested food picked up at the small intestine.
System interactions

3. The systems work together

Digestive

Breaks down food. Digested food is absorbed into the blood in the small intestine.

Respiratory

Takes in oxygen and gets rid of carbon dioxide, through the air sacs in the lungs.

Circulatory

The heart pumps blood to carry oxygen and digested food to every cell, and carbon dioxide back to the lungs.

During exercise

  • The muscles need more energy. Energy is released from digested food using oxygen (respiration).
  • So the heart rate and breathing rate both increase, to deliver more oxygen and digested food to the muscles faster, and to remove more carbon dioxide.
  • Try it in figure 2: change Activity to walking, then running. The heart rate goes 70 → 100 → 150 per minute.
Question: Why does your heart beat faster when you run?

When you run, your muscles need more energy. The heart beats faster to pump blood faster, delivering more oxygen and digested food to the muscles so more energy can be released.

Exam skills

5. Practice questions

Practice 1: Describe how oxygen from the air reaches the muscles in your leg.

Air rich in oxygen is breathed in through the nose and windpipe into the air sacs in the lungs. Oxygen passes through the air sac walls into the blood. The blood goes to the heart, which pumps it through blood vessels to the leg muscles.

Practice 2: How does digested food reach the muscles?

Digested food is absorbed into the blood in the small intestine. The blood carries it to the heart, which pumps it to the muscles.

Practice 3: After a race, Ali breathes faster for some time. Why?

His muscles needed a lot of energy and produced more carbon dioxide. Breathing faster takes in more oxygen and removes more carbon dioxide from the body.

Practice 4: Why is the heart described as a pump?

It is a muscular organ that contracts to push blood continuously through the blood vessels to all parts of the body.

Practice 5: Blood going to the lungs is rich in carbon dioxide. Where did the carbon dioxide come from?

From the cells in the rest of the body, which produce carbon dioxide when they release energy from food. The blood collects it and brings it back to the heart, which pumps it to the lungs.
