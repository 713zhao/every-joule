Energy · light and shadows

1. Light and shadows

Open full page

Key ideas

  • Light travels in straight lines.
  • A shadow forms when an object blocks light.
  • Opaque materials (card) block all light: dark shadow. Translucent materials (tracing paper) let some light through: faint shadow. Transparent materials (clear plastic) let almost all light through: almost no shadow.
  • The nearer the object is to the lamp, the bigger its shadow.

Try it

  • Switch the light on, then move the star nearer the lamp and nearer the screen. Watch the shadow size.
  • Change the material and compare the shadows.
Question: Why does the star make a bigger shadow when it is nearer the lamp?

It blocks more of the light spreading out from the lamp, because the light travels in straight lines from the lamp.

Question: Which material would you use for a window that lets light in but stops people seeing in?

A translucent material, like frosted glass.

Energy · heat flow

2. Conductors and insulators

Open full page

Key ideas

  • Heat flows from a hotter place to a colder place.
  • Metal is a good conductor of heat: heat flows through it quickly.
  • Plastic and wood are poor conductors (insulators): heat flows through them slowly.
  • The wax holding each bead melts when heat reaches it, so the bead drops.

Try it

  • Pour the hot water, then press “Wait 5 minutes” a few times. Note the order the beads drop.
Question: Which bead dropped first? Why?

The bead on the metal rod, because metal is the best conductor of heat.

Question: Why do pots have plastic or wooden handles?

Plastic and wood are poor conductors of heat, so the handle stays cool enough to hold.

Matter · solids, liquids and gases

3. Can you squash it?

Open full page

Key ideas

  • Matter is anything that has mass and takes up space.
  • A gas (air) has no fixed volume. It can be squashed (compressed) into a smaller space.
  • A liquid (water) has a fixed volume. It cannot be squashed.
  • The tips are sealed, so nothing escapes. Only the space the matter takes up changes.

Try it

  • Push the air syringe and read its volume. Then push the water syringe. Then let go.
Question: Why could the air plunger move in but not the water plunger?

Air is a gas and can be compressed. Water is a liquid with a fixed volume, so it cannot be compressed.

Question: What happens to the air when you let go? What does this tell you?

It springs back to 30 mL. Air takes up space, and the squashed air pushes the plunger back out.
