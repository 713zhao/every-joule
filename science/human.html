1. The journey of food

Open full page

Key ideas

Digestion breaks down complex food into simple, soluble substances that can be absorbed into the bloodstream. Food travels through one long tube, the alimentary canal:

Mouth→Gullet→Stomach→Small intestine→Large intestine→Anus
OrganWhat it doesDigestive juice?
MouthTeeth chew food into smaller pieces (bigger surface area). Saliva starts breaking down starch into simple sugars.Yes (saliva)
Gullet (oesophagus)Muscular tube. It squeezes to push food down to the stomach. No digestion.No
StomachChurns food with digestive juices into a thick mixture. Continues breaking down proteins. Acid kills harmful bacteria.Yes
Small intestineMore digestive juices; digestion is completed. Digested food is absorbed into the bloodstream.Yes
Large intestineNo digestion. Absorbs water and mineral salts from undigested food.No
AnusUndigested food (faeces) leaves the body.No

Physical vs chemical breakdown

2. Chewing and surface area

Open full page

Physical breakdown

Chewing (teeth) and churning (stomach) break big pieces into smaller pieces. This increases the surface area exposed to digestive juices. The food is still the same substance.

Chemical breakdown

Digestive juices break complex food down into simple, soluble substances that can enter the bloodstream.

Try it

  • Cut B and C, put the pieces in, then wait 10 minutes at a time. B (small pieces in juice) is digested twice as fast as A. C (small pieces in water) never changes.
Question: Why was B digested faster than A?

The small pieces in B have a larger total surface area than the one big piece in A, so the digestive juice could act on more of the food at the same time.

Question: What does beaker C show?

Cutting food into small pieces does not digest it. Digestive juice is needed to break the food down into simple substances.

Question: Why must A and B contain the same amount of egg white?

To make it a fair test. Only one thing, the size of the pieces, is changed.

5. Key phrases for exam answers

Surface area: “Chewing breaks food into smaller pieces, increasing the surface area for digestive juices to act on it faster.”

Complete digestion: “Digestion is completed in the small intestine, where digested food is absorbed into the bloodstream.”

Large intestine: “The large intestine absorbs excess water and mineral salts from undigested food.”

Practice 1: Why should we chew our food well before swallowing?

Chewing breaks the food into smaller pieces, increasing the surface area for digestive juices to act on, so the food is digested faster.

Practice 2: Food is not digested in the gullet. What does the gullet do?

The muscles of the gullet contract and relax to push the food down to the stomach.

Practice 3: A patient has part of the small intestine removed. Why might they get less nourishment from food?

Digestion is completed in the small intestine and the digested food is absorbed into the bloodstream there. With less small intestine, less digested food is absorbed into the bloodstream.

Practice 4: If the large intestine does not work well, the faeces become watery. Why?

The large intestine absorbs water and mineral salts from undigested food. If less water is absorbed, more water stays in the faeces.

Practice 5: Name two parts where digestive juices are added and two parts where no digestion takes place.

Juices are added in the mouth (saliva), stomach and small intestine (any two). No digestion takes place in the gullet and the large intestine (also the anus).
