RootsAnchorage: hold the plant firmly in the ground.

2. Water moves up the stem

Open full page
Key exam point: water vs food
  • Water and mineral salts travel upward only: roots → stem → leaves and flowers.
  • Food (sugar) travels in all directions: from the leaves to the stem, roots, flowers, fruits and growing tips.

Try it

  • Add red dye, wait 2 hours twice, then look inside the stem: the red water climbs up the water-carrying tubes to the leaves.
Question: Why did the celery leaves turn reddish?

The red water was taken up through the water-carrying tubes in the stem and carried up to the leaves.

3. The ringing experiment

Open full page

Outer layer removed

  • Water-carrying tubes are still there → water reaches the leaves → plant stays alive.
  • Food cannot travel down past the ring → swelling ABOVE the ring.

Inner layer cut too

  • Water-carrying tubes are cut → water cannot reach the leaves → leaves wilt and the plant dies.

Try it

  • Remove the outer layer only, wait 4 weeks, and look at the cross-section. Then press Start again and try the deep cut.
Question: Why does the swelling form above the ring and not below it?

Food is made in the leaves and travels down the food-carrying tubes. It cannot pass the ring, so it collects just above the ring.

Question: Why do the leaves stay green after the outer layer is removed?

The water-carrying tubes are not removed, so water still travels up to the leaves.

5. Key phrases for exam answers

Roots: “Roots absorb water and mineral salts from the soil and anchor the plant firmly into the ground.”

Stem: “The stem supports the leaves to trap sunlight for photosynthesis and transports water to all parts of the plant.”

Leaves: “Leaves contain chlorophyll to trap light energy to make food during photosynthesis.”

Practice 1: A plant was pulled out and replanted, but many root hairs were damaged. It wilted. Explain.

Root hairs increase the surface area for absorbing water. With fewer root hairs, less water was absorbed from the soil, so not enough water reached the leaves.

Practice 2: Why do plants kept in a dark cupboard eventually die?

Without light, the leaves cannot trap light energy to make food by photosynthesis. The plant has no food, so it dies.

Practice 3: A ring of the outer layer was removed from a tree stem. After some weeks a swelling formed above the ring. Explain.

The food-carrying tubes in the outer layer were removed. Food made in the leaves could not travel down past the ring, so it collected above the ring and caused the swelling.

Practice 4: The roots of a ringed tree eventually die, though the leaves stayed green for a while. Why?

Food from the leaves cannot travel down past the ring to the roots. The roots get no food, so they die. Then they cannot absorb water, and the whole tree dies.

Practice 5: Why do leaves of most plants grow facing the light?

So they can trap more light energy for photosynthesis, to make more food for the plant.
