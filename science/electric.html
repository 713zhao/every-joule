Circuit components · open and closed

1. Open and closed circuits

Open full page
ComponentJob
Battery / cellProvides electrical energy.
WiresConnect the components and allow electric current to flow.
SwitchOpens or closes the circuit to control the flow of current.
BulbConverts electrical energy into light (and heat) energy.

Closed circuit

A continuous, unbroken loop. Electric current flows → the bulb lights up.

Open circuit

A broken loop: an open switch, a gap or loose wire, or a blown bulb (broken filament). Current cannot flow → the bulb stays off.

Try it

  • Switch on (press the switch lever or the button). Then make a gap three ways: switch off, unclip a wire, blow the bulb. Each time, the yellow current dots stop.
Question: The switch is on and the wires are connected, but the bulb does not light. Give a possible reason.

The bulb is blown: its filament is broken, which makes a gap in the circuit, so it is an open circuit and current cannot flow. (Or: the battery is flat.)

2. Conductors and insulators

Open full page
TypeDefinitionExamples
Electrical conductorsMaterials that allow electric current to pass through them.Copper, iron, aluminium (all metals), water, graphite (pencil lead).
Electrical insulatorsMaterials that do NOT allow electric current to pass through.Rubber, plastic, glass, wood, dry paper.

Watch out

  • Graphite is not a metal, but it is a conductor.
  • Water (tap water) conducts electricity. That is why wet hands are dangerous.
  • Wires use both: a copper core to carry the current, and a plastic covering to keep us safe.

Try it

  • Test all ten objects. Sort them into conductors and insulators. Which one makes the bulb light only dimly?

Electrical safety

4. Safety rules

Danger

  1. Wet hands: never touch appliances or switches with wet hands. Water is a conductor, so you risk an electric shock.
  2. Overloaded sockets: too many plugs in one socket can overheat and cause an electrical fire.
  3. Damaged wires: if the plastic insulation is broken, the bare copper (a conductor) is exposed and can give a shock. Do not use them.
Question: Why are electrical wires covered with plastic?

Plastic is an electrical insulator. It stops electric current from passing from the copper wire into our bodies, so we do not get an electric shock.

Exam skills

6. Practice questions

Practice 1: What is the difference between an open circuit and a closed circuit?

A closed circuit is a complete, unbroken loop, so electric current can flow. An open circuit has a gap, so current cannot flow.

Practice 2: Mei Ling clips a pencil lead across the gap of a circuit tester and the bulb lights up. What can she conclude?

Pencil lead (graphite) is an electrical conductor: it allows electric current to pass through, so the circuit is closed.

Practice 3: Three bulbs are joined in series. One bulb blows. What happens to the other two? Explain.

They go off. The blown bulb has a broken filament, which makes a gap in the only path, so the circuit is open and no current flows.

Practice 4: Why are the lights in a house joined in parallel and not in series?

In parallel, each light is on its own branch. If one bulb blows, the other branches are still closed circuits, so the other lights stay on. Each light can also be switched on and off on its own, and stays at full brightness.

Practice 5: Why should you not touch a switch with wet hands?

Water is a conductor of electricity. Current could pass through the water and your body, giving you an electric shock.
