Energy · forms and conversion

1. The torch

Open full page

Key ideas

  • A battery stores chemical energy. It is used up when the torch is on.
  • Energy changes form: chemical energy → electrical energy → light and heat.
  • The switch closes a complete circuit. An open switch means no current flows.
  • Energy is not made from nothing. When the battery store is empty, the torch stops.

Try it

  • Switch on. Feel the bulb temperature rise.
  • Press “Wait 1 hour” until the battery is flat, then try switching on.
Question: Name two forms of energy that the bulb gives out.

Light energy and heat energy. The bulb also gets hot, so some energy is “wasted” as heat.

Question: Why does the bulb not light when the switch is off?

The circuit is broken, so electrical energy cannot flow from the battery to the bulb.

Systems · transport in plants

2. How water travels up a plant

Open full page

Key ideas

  • Plants take in water through the roots. Here the cut end of the stem takes in the red water.
  • Water moves up through tiny tubes in the stem (the water-carrying tubes) to the leaves.
  • The red dye shows the path: first the stem, later the veins of the leaves.

Try it

  • Add red dye, wait twice, then press “Look inside the stem”.
Question: What do the red lines in the stem show?

The tubes that carry water up from the roots to the leaves.

Question: How could you check that the dye went into the leaves?

Look for red in the veins of the leaves, or cut the stem across and look for red dots.

Matter · changes of state of water

3. Melting, boiling and condensing

Open full page

Key ideas

  • Melting: ice (solid) takes in heat and becomes water (liquid).
  • Boiling / evaporation: water (liquid) takes in heat and becomes water vapour (gas). The white puffs are tiny droplets, not the vapour itself, which you cannot see.
  • Condensation: water vapour in the air touches a cold surface, loses heat and turns back into water droplets.
  • The water level in the pot falls because water leaves as vapour.

Try it

  • Press “Heat the pot”, then “Wait 10 minutes” a few times. Compare the droplets on the glass with and without steam.
Question: Where do the droplets on the outside of the cold glass come from?

From water vapour in the air. It touches the cold glass and condenses. They do not leak through the glass.

Question: Why does the pot have less water after boiling for a long time?

Some of the water evaporated and went into the air as water vapour.
