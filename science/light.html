Light sources · how we see

1. Light sources and how we see

Open full page

Key ideas

  • A light source gives off its own light.
  • Natural light sources: the Sun, stars, lightning, fireflies.
  • Man-made light sources: lamps, torches, candles, screens.
  • The Moon and a book are not light sources. The Moon only reflects light from the Sun.
  • We see an object when light from a source falls on it and is reflected into our eyes. No light means we cannot see.

Try it

  • Switch off every light at night and see what happens to the book.
Question: Is the Moon a light source?

No. It reflects light from the Sun.

Question: Why can we not see the book in a dark room?

No light falls on the book, so no light is reflected from it into our eyes.

How light travels · speed of light

2. How light travels

Open full page

Key ideas

  • Light travels in straight lines.
  • When the holes in the cards are in a line, light passes through all of them. Move one card and the light is blocked.
  • Light is the fastest thing there is: about 300 000 km every second. It could go around the Earth about 7 times in one second.
  • Light from the Sun takes about 8 minutes to reach the Earth.

Try it

  • Switch on, then slide the middle card. Then press “Slow motion” to watch a pulse of light (in real life the trip takes about 3 billionths of a second).
Question: What happens when the middle card is moved? Why?

The light stops at the middle card. Light travels in straight lines and cannot bend around the card.

Question: Which do we notice first in a thunderstorm, lightning or thunder? Why?

Lightning. Light travels much faster than sound.

Reflection

3. Reflection

Open full page

Key ideas

  • Light bounces off surfaces. This is called reflection.
  • A smooth, shiny surface like a mirror reflects light in one direction. The light bounces off at the same angle it hits the mirror.
  • Rough surfaces like white paper scatter light in many directions.
  • Dark surfaces like black paper take in (absorb) most of the light.

Try it

  • Turn the mirror until the reflected light hits the target. Watch “angle in” and “angle out”.
  • Change the surface to white paper and black paper.
Question: How many times did you turn the mirror clockwise to hit the target?

Five times (25 degrees). Then the angle in and angle out are both 48 degrees.

Question: Name two uses of mirrors.

Car side mirrors, periscopes, dental mirrors, bathroom mirrors.

Shadows · materials

4. Light and shadows

Open full page

Key ideas

  • A shadow forms when an object blocks light.
  • Nearer the lamp, the shadow is bigger.
  • Opaque objects make dark shadows; translucent objects make faint shadows; transparent objects make almost none.

Try it

  • Move the star and change the material.
Question: How can you make a shadow smaller?

Move the object nearer the screen (further from the lamp).

Question: Why does clear plastic make almost no shadow?

It is transparent, so it lets almost all light through.

Colours of light

5. Colours of light

Open full page

Key ideas

  • White light is a mix of colours.
  • A glass prism bends light. Each colour bends a different amount, so they spread out: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet.
  • A rainbow forms the same way, when sunlight passes through raindrops.
  • A coloured filter lets only its own colour through.

Try it

  • Switch on to see the rainbow, take away the prism, then try each colour filter.
Question: Which colour bends the most? Which bends the least?

Violet bends the most; red bends the least.

Question: When do we see a rainbow in the sky?

When the Sun is behind us and there are raindrops in front of us, often just after rain.
